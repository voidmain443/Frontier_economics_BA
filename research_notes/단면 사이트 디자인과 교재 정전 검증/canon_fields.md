# Field-course canon for 단면 Books 11–22: verification of the Gemini list and a reading list for authors (as of 2026-11-16)

Scope. These notes fact-check every field-course item in the Gemini report and add what it missed. They cover the 12 field volumes (Books 11–22) of the 단면 / Cross-Section series. For each book they give the global classic canon, next-generation and open-access material (2022–2026 emphasis), Korean standard texts, representative syllabi, and what the book should borrow or avoid. The chapter lists referred to below are in `docs/books/11-trade.md` … `docs/books/22-method.md`.

Method. The 12 books were split across four parallel checks (11–13, 14–16, 17–19, 20–22). I then ran lead spot-checks on the riskiest verdicts ("no newer edition found"), and those results are folded in below.

Verdict key:
- **CONFIRMED**: the claim holds; the latest edition details are added.
- **WRONG**: the claim is incorrect or out of date; the correct fact is given.
- **UNVERIFIABLE**: no live source confirms the claim, so it is listed under Gaps.

Source caveats:
- Each researcher exhausted the shared web-search budget partway through. Later checks were direct fetches of publisher, RePEc/IDEAS, Crossref, MIT OCW, LSE course-guide, Aladin/YES24 and Bank of Korea pages.
- These sites returned HTTP 403 or blocked the fetch: MIT Press, Cambridge UP (some pages), ScienceDirect, Annual Reviews, nobelprize.org, Uribe's Columbia pages and Ray's NYU page. Facts from those publishers therefore rest on RePEc, Crossref, retailer or library records, and each line says which.
- Where a fact rests only on a search-engine snippet, the line says so.

## 0. Cross-cutting: which Gemini claims hold, and what changes the reading list

### Takeaway
Of 103 Gemini claims checked, **90 are CONFIRMED, 5 are WRONG and 8 are UNVERIFIABLE**. The larger risk is staleness. About 20 of the confirmed items now have a newer edition, a new co-author, a new publisher, or a Korean title different from the one Gemini gave. Several "newer editions" that Gemini floated could not be found anywhere: Hausman anthology 4th ed., Reiss 2nd ed., Angner 4th ed., *Handbook of Behavioral Economics* vol. 3. Do not cite them.

### Inferences

#### Citation policy for the series
- Cite each canon item as edition + year + publisher. Re-check the following before press, because they are in flux:
  - KOM (still 12th)
  - Mishkin (still 13th)
  - Feenstra-Taylor (6th, ©2027)
  - Todaro (14th listed for 2027)
  - Ray (revision announced)
  - BKM (yearly "Releases")
  - Kolstad (3rd, 2026)
  - Gruber (8th, 2026)
- Drop Hausman 4th, Reiss 2nd, Angner 4th and *Handbook of Behavioral Economics* vol. 3 from any list unless a publisher page appears.

#### The series' niche in Korea
- Among the Korean standard texts verified here, only 박기영 『금융경제학』 (2026, Python) visibly builds in code. None advertises falsification conditions or a research-design slot. This is inferred from listings and tables of contents, not from reading the books.
- The 단면 format (held-fixed values, falsification, Korean data, computation) is therefore differentiated. Its closest existing models are the paper-based course designs above (Saez, MIT 14.771, Toronto ECO 310, LSE EH237), not any textbook.

### Inferences
#### Borrow / avoid for 단면 Book 11
- **Borrow ACR (2012) and Costinot & Rodríguez-Clare (2014) as the "computation" spine.** Gains from trade then depend on two measurable numbers: the domestic expenditure share and a trade elasticity. That gives every chapter an explicit "held fixed (with values)" line and a falsifiable welfare number that can be computed from BOK IO tables. (Inference.)
- **Borrow the ADH/CDP research design for "who loses inside the country."** Regional exposure to import competition, then local employment and wage outcomes. Korean analogue: KOSIS regional and industry employment against Chinese import penetration. This is better than KOM's specific-factors diagrams, which predict the sign but not the size. (Inference.)
- **Borrow tariff pass-through evidence (Amiti-Redding-Weinstein 2019; Fajgelbaum et al. 2020) as a falsification exercise.** Terms-of-trade theory predicts partial pass-through for a large country; the 2018 data showed near-complete pass-through to US import prices. Feenstra-Taylor 6e is the only textbook updated for the 2025–26 tariffs. (Inference; check the 6e content directly.)
- **Borrow value-added trade accounting (Johnson-Noguera; KWW).** It fits Korea's exporter profile and the BOK IO tables directly. (Inference.)
- **Avoid copying KOM/Salvatore chapter order.** Both teach Ricardo/HO/specific-factors as diagrams with no parameter values, no code and no Korean data. The Korean translations of Feenstra-Taylor are two editions behind (3e vs 6e). (Inference.)

### Inferences
#### Borrow / avoid for 단면 Book 12
- **Borrow SGU's calibrated-model format.** Each chapter runs model, then calibration table, then simulated moments compared with data, with exercises, code and published solutions. This maps one-to-one onto 단면's "held fixed (with values) / computation / falsification" slots. Use the SOE-RBC chapter with Korean ECOS data. (Inference.)
- **Make Meese-Rogoff (1983) and Engel-West (2005) the standing falsification test for "exchange rate as asset price."** Any fundamentals model must beat a random walk out of sample. Run this on KRW/USD from ECOS. (Inference.)
- **Borrow DCP (Gopinath et al. 2020) and dollar-funding work (Ivashina-Scharfstein-Stein 2015) for Korean relevance.** Dollar invoicing and dollar funding are what make KRW depreciation pass through to import prices and to bank funding stress. Korean customs invoicing data would make a natural evidence box. (Inference; the data source is not verified here.)
- **Use Brunnermeier & Reis (2023) as the crisis-chapter template.** Ten short chapters, each with one mechanism and one episode. Use Forbes-Warnock episode coding with Rey's global financial cycle for the capital-flow-network chapters. (Inference.)
- **Avoid making Obstfeld-Rogoff (1996) the core.** It predates financial frictions, sudden-stop and macroprudential analysis, and has no code. Keep it as the reference for intertemporal current-account derivations. Also avoid the KOM/Salvatore undergraduate apparatus (DD-AA-style diagrams), which offers no parameter values. (Inference.)

### Inferences
#### Borrow / avoid for 단면 Book 13
- **Avoid the textbook money multiplier as a causal mechanism.** Teach it only as a falsified model. JEE has run three critiques (Neveu 2020; Hook 2022; Ihrig-Wolla 2022), BoE 2014 rejects "multiplying up", and the Fed's own educators wrote "R.I.P." in 2021. Before adopting Mishkin 13e or Wright, check how much of the money-supply chapter rests on the multiplier (Wright's ch. 15 title confirms it is present there). (Inference.)
- **Borrow CORE 2.0 §6.8 / BoE 2014 balance-sheet T-accounts as the "what moves / what is held fixed" device.** Loan → deposit → reserve settlement, with the policy rate held fixed. These T-accounts can be filled directly from BOK and OpenDART bank balance sheets. (Inference.)
- **Borrow Diamond-Dybvig as the model and Goldstein-Pauzner as the falsifiable extension.** Global games pin down a run *probability* as a function of fundamentals. That gives testable comparative statics (deposit insurance coverage, liquidity ratios) for a Korean case such as the 2011 savings-bank runs. Korean case data were not checked here. (Inference.)
- **Borrow network-contagion models (Allen-Gale 2000; Acemoglu et al. 2015; Elliott-Golub-Jackson 2014) for the contagion chapters.** They produce explicit thresholds that students can compute on a small interbank matrix. Use Freixas-Rochet for the theory proofs. Use Gorton (repo runs) and 14.454's March 2023 readings for evidence. (Inference.)
- **Korean relevance: the BOK series are the primary institutional sources but several are dated.** 『한국의 통화정책』 is the 2017 edition and 『한국의 거시건전성정책』 is from 2015, so chapters must flag post-2017 changes rather than copy the BOK framing. 박기영 (2026) is the only Korean Python-based text found; review it before relying on it. (Inference.)

#### Borrow / avoid for 단면 Book 15
- **Borrow QuantEcon's McCall lecture as the template for "held fixed, with values" plus computation.** It states every parameter (c = 25, β = 0.99, offer distribution), produces a number (reservation wage about 47.32), and ships runnable code. Re-run it with Korean 실업급여 replacement levels as c.

#### Borrow / avoid for 단면 Book 16
- **Borrow ECO 310's proof of feasibility.** An undergraduate course can teach production functions, differentiated-product demand, competition and entry with real data and software and no textbook. ### Inferences
#### Borrow / avoid for 단면 Book 17
- **Borrow** the research-design spine of MIT 14.771/14.73: every chapter pairs a mechanism (for example a poverty trap) with an RCT or quasi-experiment, and a replication task carries real weight in the grade. This maps directly onto 단면's "evidence with research design" slot. Poverty traps open 14.73, which matches our "relax A5" framing.
- **Borrow** de Janvry & Sadoulet's Excel/Stata exercise model and Jones & Vollrath's one-script-per-figure Python repository, so every figure in Book 17 can be regenerated. Use ECOS/KOSIS series where they replace Penn World Table data for Korea.
- **Borrow** Korea as the natural experiment that *Why Nations Fail* already uses (North vs South). Pair it with KDI 『한국경제 60년사』 and the KSP modules for the policy record, so falsification exercises can use Korean episodes such as HCI drive timing and land reform.
- **Avoid** taking *Poor Economics* and *Good Economics* as the theoretical core. Reviewers note they are popular-science level with "hardly anything in the way of theory" ([dannyreviews](https://dannyreviews.com/h/Poor_Economics.html)). Use them for evidence vignettes only, and cite the 2025 revised edition.
- **Avoid** fixing editions too early. Ray (revision under way), Todaro (14th ed. listed for 2027) and Weil (moved to Ashraf & Weil at Routledge) are all in transition. Cite Ashraf & Weil 4th ed. now, and re-check Ray and Todaro before going to press.
- **Gap to fill ourselves:** no Korean in-print text titled 『개발경제학』 appears to exist, and 강성진 3판 (2026) is the newest Korean 경제발전론. There is room for a Korean text built around research design.

- **DICE-2023 parameters, as stated in the NBER WP text I extracted:**
  - Pure rate of social time preference ρ = **1.0%/yr** (down from 1.5% in DICE-2016).

### Inferences
#### Borrow / avoid for 단면 Book 20
- **Borrow the LSE EC310 split** ("departures from preferences" vs "departures from rational expectations"). It maps directly onto "relax A6": each chapter states which axiom is dropped and which ones are still held fixed.
- **Borrow the MIT 14.13 approach of papers plus problem sets.** Each chapter's evidence block should cite the original design (lab, field or administrative data) rather than a textbook summary. Use Angner 3e, which has a Korean translation, as students' companion reading. Use Dhami (2016, 1,798 pp.) only as the formal reference for statements and proofs.
- **Make falsification concrete with ex-ante forecasts** (DellaVigna, Pope & Vivalt 2019). Students predict an effect size before seeing the result. Then contrast academic nudge trials with DellaVigna & Linos (2022) and with Maier et al. (2022) on publication bias. This builds replication-crisis awareness into the format.
- **Avoid retracted or fragile flagship examples**, such as the honesty-pledge "signing first" result, retracted in 2021. Treat popular books (Thaler 2015, Kahneman 2011, Ariely 2009) as motivation, not as evidence. Each "evidence" block should name a replication or meta-analysis status.
- **Korean data hook (my suggestion, not checked against any source):** defaults and inertia in retirement saving (Madrian & Shea 2001 is the classic) could be paired with Korean pension or default-option data. Any such dataset still needs to be verified.

### Inferences
#### Borrow / avoid for 단면 Book 21
- **Present the big theses as competing hypotheses with falsification conditions:** geography (Allen), culture and selection (Clark), the culture of growth (Mokyr), institutions, and demography (Galor's unified growth theory). ### Inferences
#### Borrow / avoid for 단면 Book 22
- **Use the LSE PH311/PH434 topic list as the chapter skeleton:** utility measurement, behavioural challenges, idealised models, measurement such as inflation, RCTs, microfoundations, crisis prediction. Students would submit a mock replication package built from ECOS, KOSIS or OpenDART data.
- **Use the publishing-economics papers as data exercises, not just reading:** Card & DellaVigna (2013, 2020), Card et al. (2020) on gender, and Hamermesh (2013) on authorship. Students can test, for example, whether citation counts track editor decisions, as a falsifiable claim about peer review.
- **Put replication and p-hacking evidence (Chang & Li 2022; Brodeur et al. 2020) in the methods chapter.** It gives empirical content to "falsification conditions" in every chapter of the series.
- **Avoid assuming a Korean methodology literature exists to lean on.** Korean coverage is sparse, and the older Blaug and 송현호 texts (1992) are out of print. The series will need its own Korean terminology and should cite 홍태희 (2024) as the current local reference.
