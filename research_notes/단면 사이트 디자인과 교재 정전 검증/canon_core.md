# Canon verification for the 10 core books of 단면 / Cross-Section (as of 16 Nov 2026)

Scope note for the report writer. This pass checked editions, years, publishers and open-access status against publisher pages, author pages, the CORE and QuantEcon sites, and the 알라딘 catalogue (for Korean books). Partway through, the session's shared web-search budget ran out. Publisher and author pages could still be fetched directly, but some candidate additions could no longer be searched for. Those are listed under each book's **Gaps** as "not verified this pass". They are not presented as facts. Items marked "canonical, not re-verified" are standard references whose bibliographic details were not re-checked online in this pass.

Verdict legend: **CONFIRMED** (primary or near-primary source agrees), **WRONG** (the correct fact is given), **UNVERIFIABLE** (no reliable source found, or the fetch failed). **PARTLY** means one part of the claim is confirmed and another part is not.

## Q0. Which of the Gemini report's claims hold up?

### Takeaway
Most of the Gemini list holds up. There are seven material errors: Krugman & Wells is in its 7th edition, not the 6th. Goolsbee–Levitt–Syverson's 4th edition is ©2024, not 2025. Osborne & Rubinstein's *A Course in Game Theory* has no 2023 second edition; that edition belongs to their *Models in Microeconomic Theory*. Bruce Hansen's *Probability and Statistics for Economists* is not free. Blanchard is in its 9th edition, and the current Korean translation is 9판 from 한빛아카데미 (2026), not 시그마프레스. Ljungqvist–Sargent's latest edition is the 4th, Galí's is the 2nd, and no 5th edition of Walsh was found. 『현대경제학원론』 8판 now lists five authors. Two items could not be confirmed at all: Rubinstein's "Ten Q&A" and 조인구 as a co-author of 왕규호's 『게임이론』.

## Book 1 — Economic reasoning: models, conditions, evidence (12 ch)

### Takeaway
No single global canon exists for a first-year methodology-and-writing course. The usable stack combines Rodrik (models as conditional fables), Rubinstein's free *Economic Fables* and the O–R *Models* definition of "model", McCloskey for prose, Head for paper introductions, and Huntington-Klein or Cunningham for causal design. ### Inferences
- **Borrow:** Rubinstein's and O–R's insistence that a model is a stated set of assumptions, not a forecast. This is the "conditional" framing the series wants: what moves, and what is held fixed. Take Head's five-part introduction as the template for each chapter's writing exercise, and McCloskey's rules as the style sheet. Use *The Effect*'s DAG-first treatment for causality chapters; its partial-identification chapter is a natural bridge to "falsification conditions".

Use them as background reading for the author, not as models for a first-year chapter. Shapiro (2026) is graduate-level; borrow its "connect models to data" framing, not its level.
- **Opportunity:** No Korean book covers this ground, so Book 1 can be distinctive.

### Inferences
- **Borrow:** QuantEcon's executable-lecture format (MyST/Jupyter, Python plus JAX), paired with Stachurski–Sargent's theorem-then-algorithm pairing. This fits the series' "math basis + computation" columns exactly. The Xiamen partnership is a precedent for a Korean-language QuantEcon-style localisation.
- Order chapters by solution concept: fixed point and contraction (VFI), then projection and perturbation, then heterogeneous agents (Aiyagari/HACT), then deep learning (DEQN/PINN, Scheidegger), then RL (Moll), then ABM (Axtell–Farmer). Each step relaxes something the previous step held fixed.
- **Avoid:** MATLAB-only or Fortran-only code (Judd- and Miranda–Fackler-era). Avoid presenting deep learning without convergence or stability caveats; Scheidegger himself calls the notes a "subjective, incomplete snapshot".
