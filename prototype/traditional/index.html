Cross-Section

An economics textbook draws the world as flat slices: one market with everything else held still, one household standing for millions. This project asks what those slices are cut from, what they cannot show, and how economics could be taught without them.

  1. 1 The classical textbook The undergraduate sequence as one story in eight stages, from one market to the eleven field courses. Each stage adds an agent or a market, and every figure is a slice of something larger. Fold the page, and each figure raises, step by step, the dimension it was drawn without, over a picture of the whole economy that each stage builds further.
  2. 2 Unlike and connected What the classical figures cannot draw, because in them every agent of a kind is alike and nobody is connected to anybody in particular: who is connected to whom, chains rather than cores, no typical size, how far people reason, and how failures travel between banks. Then the projects rebuilding the textbook around them: CORE, QuantEcon and others.
  3. 3 A new archive A proposal for teaching classical and modern economics together. The archive is arranged by the models a textbook teaches rather than by course titles. Each model is a state of one wheel, the circular flow of the whole economy: what it moves, what it holds still, what it leaves out. Five relations and eight assumptions join the models, and five ways lead through them to every textbook.
